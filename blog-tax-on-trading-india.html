How is intraday & F&O trading taxed in India?

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

Taxes confuse almost every new trader in India. This is a plain-English overview to help you understand the categories — not tax advice. For your actual filing, please consult a qualified Chartered Accountant.

Intraday equity = speculative business income

Profits from intraday equity (buying and selling the same stock the same day, without delivery) are generally treated as speculative business income. It's added to your total income and taxed at your slab rate. Losses here are speculative losses, which can usually only be set off against speculative gains.

F&O = non-speculative business income

Profits from futures and options are generally treated as non-speculative business income, also taxed at your slab rate. The upside: F&O losses can usually be set off against most other income heads (except salary) and carried forward — if you file on time.

Delivery trades = capital gains

If you take delivery and sell later, it's capital gains — short-term (held ≤12 months) or long-term, each with its own rate. This is a different bucket from your intraday/F&O activity.

Turnover, audit and why records matter

Because intraday and F&O are "business income," concepts like turnover and a possible tax audit can apply depending on your numbers. The thread running through all of it: you need clean, complete records of every trade. Traders who kept a proper journal breeze through filing; those who didn't scramble.
