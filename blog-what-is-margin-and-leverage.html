What is margin and leverage in trading?

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

Margin and leverage let you control a large position with a small amount of money. That sounds great until you realise they amplify your losses exactly as much as your gains. Understanding them is non-negotiable before trading F&O.

Margin

Margin is the money your broker requires you to put up to open a leveraged position — like a deposit. For index futures and option selling, this runs into lakhs per lot and rises with volatility.

Leverage

Leverage is the multiplier. With 5x leverage, ₹20,000 controls ₹1,00,000 of exposure. A 10% move makes or loses ₹10,000 — that's 50% of your ₹20,000. Leverage turns normal moves into account-sized swings.

Margin calls

If a leveraged trade moves against you and your margin drops too low, the broker issues a margin call — add funds or get squared off automatically, often at the worst possible moment. This is how over-leveraged accounts vanish in one bad session.

Why leverage is the fastest way to blow up

Leverage doesn't make a bad strategy good — it makes it fatal faster. The survivors use less leverage than they're offered, size by risk not margin, and always trade with a stop-loss.
