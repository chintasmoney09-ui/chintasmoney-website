Support and resistance explained

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

Support and resistance are the most useful idea in charting — and the most abused. Done right, they give you logical places to enter and, more importantly, to put your stop. Done wrong, they become lines you draw to justify a trade you already wanted.

The simple definition

  • Support: a price area where buying has repeatedly stepped in and price stopped falling.
  • Resistance: a price area where selling has repeatedly stepped in and price stopped rising.

Think zones, not exact lines. Markets respect areas, not pixels.

How to spot them

Look for prices the chart has turned at more than once — previous swing highs/lows, round numbers, the day's open. The more touches, the more meaningful. A level that broke often flips role: old resistance becomes new support.

How disciplined traders use them

The biggest value isn't the entry — it's the stop. If you're long off support, your stop goes just below it: that's where your idea is proven wrong. This ties your stop placement to the chart and lets you size the position properly.

Levels are tools; discipline is the edge

Support and resistance give you a map, not a guarantee — price breaks levels all the time. What protects you is honouring the stop and sizing small. ChintasMoney measures whether you actually do that, trade after trade.
