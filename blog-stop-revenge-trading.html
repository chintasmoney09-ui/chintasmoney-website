How to stop revenge trading & FOMO

Reading time: ~3 min · Educational — not investment advice

One red trade rarely ruins a day. What ruins it is the trade you take right after — bigger, unplanned, to "get it back." That's revenge trading. Its twin is FOMO: jumping into a move you didn't plan because you can't bear to miss it. Both come from the same place — emotion overriding process — and both turn a small, normal loss into a spiral.

Why it happens

After a loss, your brain wants to erase the pain now. So you size up and drop your rules, hunting a quick win. The market doesn't know or care that you're owed one. The result is usually a second loss, which makes the urge stronger — and the loop tightens.

Rules that actually break the loop

  • The 10-minute rule. After any red trade, step away for 10 minutes. No screens. The urge fades fast once the adrenaline drops.
  • A daily loss limit. Decide in advance: after X losing trades or ₹Y down, you're done for the day. Write it down before the session.
  • Pre-trade check. Before every entry, confirm it's a planned setup with a stop-loss and sensible size. If it fails the check, it's not a trade — it's an emotion.
  • Never size up to recover. The trade after a loss should be the same size or smaller, never bigger.

Spot it in your own log

The fastest cure is seeing the pattern in your own data. When you log trades honestly — including why you exited and how you felt — the revenge and FOMO trades stand out: they cluster right after losses, they skip the stop-loss, and they lose more often. Once you can see the leak, it's much easier to plug. That's the whole idea behind a Discipline Score: it counts these moments so you can't pretend they didn't happen.
