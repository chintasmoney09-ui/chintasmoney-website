Risk management in trading: the position-sizing guide

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

Ask any trader who survived their first few years what mattered most and they'll say the same thing: risk management. Not entries, not indicators — how much they lost when they were wrong. Here's the core of it, in plain maths.

Rule 1: Decide your risk before your reward

Before a trade, the only number you control is how much you'll lose if it fails. Cap it. The common standard is 1% of capital per trade — so on ₹1,00,000, your max loss per trade is ₹1,000. Ten losses in a row and you're down 10%, not wiped out. That's the whole point.

Rule 2: Size the position to the stop

Position size isn't "how many lots can I afford" — it's "how many lots keep my loss at 1%." The formula:

Quantity = (Capital × risk %) ÷ (stop-loss distance per unit)

Wider stop → smaller size. Tighter stop → larger size. Our position size calculator does this instantly.

Rule 3: Think in R, not rupees

Your risk on a trade is "1R." If you risk ₹1,000 to make ₹2,000, that's a 2R trade (a 1:2 risk-reward). Thinking in R frees you from the rupee emotion and keeps you honest: a strategy that wins 40% of the time is still profitable at 1:2. Check any trade with the risk:reward calculator.

Rule 4: Protect the account first

  • Daily loss limit: stop trading after a set loss for the day.
  • Never average a loser: adding to a losing trade turns one mistake into a disaster.
  • No moving stops wider: the stop is a promise to yourself. Keep it.
