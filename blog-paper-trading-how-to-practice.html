Paper trading: how to practice before risking real money

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

Paper trading means placing trades on paper (or a simulator) with no real money — a way to test a strategy and learn mechanics without losing cash. Done right it's useful; done lazily it builds false confidence.

How to paper trade properly

  1. Treat it like real money. Fixed capital, real position sizes, real stops. "Unlimited pretend money" teaches nothing.
  2. Follow a written plan. Same setup, risk and rules you'd use live. See how to make a trading plan.
  3. Log every trade — entry, stop, exit reason, emotion — exactly as you would live.
  4. Review weekly. Find your leaks before real money is on the line.

The one big limitation

Paper trading can't replicate emotion. With nothing at stake, you hold winners calmly and cut losers cleanly — then real money arrives and fear and greed take over. That's the gap that surprises everyone.

Bridge to real money the safe way

When you go live, start with tiny size so emotion is introduced gradually, risk ≤1% per trade, and keep journaling. ChintasMoney scores your discipline on both practice and real trades, so you can see whether your behaviour survives the switch. Also read how much money to start with.
