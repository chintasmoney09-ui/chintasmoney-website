Options trading for beginners in India

Reading time: ~6 min · Educational — not investment advice

Options are powerful and genuinely risky — SEBI's data shows most individual F&O traders lose money. This is a plain-English starting point so you at least understand what you're doing before risking a rupee. No tips, no calls — just the basics done honestly.

What is an option?

An option is a contract that gives you the right (not the obligation) to buy or sell an underlying — like Nifty or a stock — at a fixed price, before a fixed date. You pay a premium for that right.

Calls vs puts

  • Call option: gains value when the underlying goes up.
  • Put option: gains value when the underlying goes down.

As a buyer, your maximum loss is the premium you paid. As a seller (writer), you collect the premium but your risk can be far larger — which is why beginners should not sell options until they deeply understand the risk.

The words you must know

  • Strike price: the fixed price in the contract.
  • Premium: what you pay (or receive) for the option.
  • Expiry: the date the contract ends. Indian index options expire weekly/monthly.
  • Lot size: options trade in lots — see the Nifty & Bank Nifty lot sizes.

Why most beginners lose at options

Options decay in value over time (theta), so even a "right" view can lose if it's slow. Add leverage, weekly expiries and emotion, and it becomes a fast way to lose money. The ones who survive treat the first months as learning, risk ≤1% per trade, and never chase.
