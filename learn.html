Trading Discipline 101

A plain-English guide for Indian F&O, intraday & swing traders. No tips, no jargon — just the behaviour that actually decides your P&L.

Why your P&L lies

A green day can come from a reckless, oversized, no-stop-loss trade that happened to work. A red day can come from a perfectly disciplined trade that simply didn't hit. If you judge yourself only by profit & loss, you reward luck and punish good process. Over hundreds of trades, process — not any single outcome — is what compounds. That's why we score your behaviour, not your P&L.

What is a Discipline Score?

A single 0–100 number built from your own logged trades. It rewards the habits that keep traders alive and penalises the ones that blow accounts:

  • Did you set and respect a stop-loss?
  • Did you exit on plan (target/stop) rather than on emotion?
  • Did you size risk sensibly instead of betting the account?
  • Did you avoid overtrading, revenge and FOMO?

The 5 leaks that kill accounts

🚫

No stop-loss

Trading without a pre-decided exit turns a small loss into an account-ending one. A stop is not optional.

😤

Revenge trading

Firing back after a loss to "win it back" is how one red trade becomes five. Step away for 10 minutes.

🐑

FOMO entries

Chasing a move you missed means buying late, near the top, with no plan. Boredom is not a setup.

🔁

Overtrading

More trades ≠ more profit — just more brokerage and more mistakes. The best traders sit on their hands.

✂️

Cutting winners early

Fear makes you book tiny gains while letting losers run. Let winners reach target; trust the plan you set.

Position sizing & the 1% rule

Risk decides survival. If you risk just 1% of capital per trade, you can be wrong 20 times in a row and still have ~80% of your account. Risk 10% and a single bad streak ends you. Position size is simply: (capital × risk%) ÷ (entry − stop). Decide it before you click — our free risk calculator does the maths in seconds.

A quick glossary

  • Stop-loss (SL): the price at which you exit a losing trade, decided in advance.
  • Risk : Reward (R:R): how much you stand to make vs lose. A 1:2 R:R keeps you profitable even at a 40% win rate.
  • R-multiple: a trade's result measured in units of your initial risk (e.g. +2R means you made twice what you risked).
  • Drawdown: the drop from your account's peak to its trough — the pain you must survive.
  • Position size: how many units/lots you take, set by your risk, not your conviction.
  • Win rate: the share of trades that are profitable. High win rate with poor R:R can still lose money.
