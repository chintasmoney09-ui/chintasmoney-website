How much money do you need to start F&O trading in India?

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

It's the first question almost every beginner asks — and the honest answer isn't a single number. You can technically start option buying with a few thousand rupees, but "can start" and "should start" are very different. Here's a realistic breakdown for India.

The technical minimum

For option buying, you only need the premium. One out-of-the-money index option can cost a few hundred to a few thousand rupees per lot — so ₹5,000–₹15,000 is enough to place trades. For futures or option selling, you need span + exposure margin, which for index contracts typically runs into roughly ₹1–1.5 lakh per lot and changes with volatility.

The number that actually matters

Capital isn't about entry — it's about survival. If your account is so small that one or two normal losses wipe it out, you'll never last long enough to get good. A useful frame: pick an amount where risking 1% per trade is still a meaningful position, and losing it entirely would not hurt your life. For most beginners that means treating the first chunk as tuition, not investment.

Why "start small" beats "start big"

  • SEBI data is brutal: around 93% of individual F&O traders lose money. Starting big just means losing big.
  • Skill compounds, capital doesn't — yet. Your edge is built in the first 100–200 trades. Build it cheaply.
  • Emotion scales with money. A ₹10,000 loss and a ₹1,00,000 loss trigger very different decisions. Learn to behave at small size first.

A sensible starting plan

  1. Begin with money you can fully afford to lose.
  2. Risk ≤1% per trade; never add to a loser.
  3. Log every trade and track your discipline score, not just P&L.
  4. Scale up capital only after your behaviour is consistent — not after one good week.

Capital keeps you in; discipline makes you money

Two traders with the same ₹50,000 get completely different outcomes — the difference is behaviour.
