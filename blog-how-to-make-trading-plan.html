How to make a trading plan that you'll actually follow

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

A trading plan is simply your rules, written down before the pressure hits. It's the difference between trading and gambling. You don't need a 20-page document — you need a one-pager you'll actually follow. Here's how to build it.

1. Define your setup

What specific, repeatable condition makes you enter? "It looked like it would go up" is not a setup. "Breakout above the opening range with rising volume" is. If you can't describe it in one sentence, you don't have one yet.

2. Fix your risk per trade

Decide the single number first: how much you'll lose if wrong. 1% of capital is a solid default. Everything else sizes around this. See the 1% rule.

3. Pre-set entry, stop and target

  • Entry: the exact trigger that puts you in.
  • Stop-loss: where you're proven wrong — set before entry, never widened.
  • Target: ideally at least 1:2 risk-reward. Check with the risk:reward calculator.

4. Write your hard rules

These are your non-negotiables: daily loss limit, max trades per day, no revenge trading, no averaging losers. This is where plans are won or lost. Borrow from the 10 intraday rules.

5. A simple one-page template

My setup: ______ · Risk per trade: __% · Entry trigger: ______ · Stop: ______ · Target (min 1:2): ______ · Daily loss limit: ₹______ · Max trades/day: ___ · Rules I will NOT break: ______
