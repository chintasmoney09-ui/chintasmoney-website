How to stop overtrading

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

Overtrading — taking too many trades, often low-quality ones — is one of the quietest account-killers. Each extra trade adds fees, taxes and another chance to make a mistake. More trades almost never means more profit.

Why you overtrade

  • Boredom: a quiet market feels like "missing out," so you force a trade. Boredom is not a setup.
  • Revenge: after a loss you fire back to win it back — see how to stop revenge trading.
  • FOMO: every green candle feels like a train leaving without you.
  • Action addiction: clicking feels productive even when it's destructive.

How to actually stop

  1. Set a max trades/day limit. When it's hit, you're done — no exceptions.
  2. Only take A+ setups from your written plan. If it's not on the plan, it's not a trade.
  3. Use a daily loss limit to stop the spiral early.
  4. Schedule breaks. Step away during the midday dead zone when overtrading peaks.
  5. Count your trades. Awareness alone reduces the habit.
