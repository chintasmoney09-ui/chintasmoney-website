Nifty & Bank Nifty lot size (2026)

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

If you trade index options or futures in India, the lot size decides how much one contract moves your account. Get it wrong and a "small" trade can carry a far bigger risk than you think. Here are the current index lot sizes, what they really cost, and how to size a position so one bad trade doesn't end your month.

Current index F&O lot sizes

These are the standard index lot sizes used across NSE and BSE derivatives. Exchanges revise lot sizes periodically — always confirm the live contract on your broker before you trade.

  • Nifty 50 — 75 units per lot
  • Bank Nifty — 35 units per lot
  • FinNifty (Nifty Financial Services) — 65 units per lot
  • Nifty Midcap Select — 120 units per lot
  • Nifty Next 50 — 25 units per lot
  • Sensex — 20 units per lot
  • Bankex — 30 units per lot

Stock F&O lots differ per stock and change often, so check each symbol's contract on your broker.

What one lot actually costs you

For futures, your exposure is roughly lot size × index level. If Bank Nifty is near 52,000, one futures lot controls about 35 × 52,000 ≈ ₹18 lakh of exposure — even though your margin is far less. For options, your maximum loss as a buyer is the premium × lot size; as a seller it can be much larger. The point: one lot is rarely "small."

The mistake that wrecks accounts: sizing by margin, not by risk

Most blow-ups come from buying as many lots as the margin allows, then watching a normal move erase the account. The fix is to size by how much you're willing to lose on the trade, not by what you can afford to buy. A simple rule: risk no more than 1% of your capital per trade, and let that — plus your stop distance — decide the number of lots.

A quick, safe way to size

  1. Decide your rupee risk for the trade (e.g. 1% of capital).
  2. Set your stop-loss distance in points.
  3. Lots ≈ (rupee risk) ÷ (stop points × lot size).
