The 1% risk rule: how to size every trade

Reading time: ~3 min · Educational — not investment advice

Most blown F&O accounts don't die from bad analysis. They die from one oversized trade. The fix is boring and powerful: decide, before you enter, the maximum you're willing to lose on a trade — and never risk more. The common version is the 1% rule: risk no more than 1–2% of your capital on any single trade.

Why 1%?

Because losing streaks are normal. Even a good strategy can lose 5–6 in a row. At 1% risk per trade, six losses in a row costs you ~6% — annoying, survivable, recoverable. At 20% risk per trade, three losses nearly wipe you out. Small risk is what lets you stay in the game long enough for your edge to show up.

The formula

Position size follows from three numbers you already know:

  1. Capital — say ₹1,00,000.
  2. Risk per trade — 1% = ₹1,000 (your max loss).
  3. Stop distance — entry minus stop-loss, per unit.

Quantity = Risk amount ÷ Stop distance.
Entry ₹100, stop ₹95 → ₹5 risk per unit → ₹1,000 ÷ ₹5 = 200 units. For F&O, round down to whole lots.

Change any input and the size changes automatically. A wider stop means a smaller position — the risk stays fixed at ₹1,000. That's the whole point: your loss is capped by design, not by hope.

The habit that makes it stick

Calculate size before you enter, every time — never after you're already in and emotional. A risk calculator turns this into a 5-second step. Do it for 20 trades and it becomes automatic; that single habit is worth more than any tip.
