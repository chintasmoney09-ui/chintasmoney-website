Candlestick patterns for beginners

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

A candlestick packs four prices into one shape: open, high, low and close. Read in context they hint at who's winning — buyers or sellers. Read as magic signals, they'll bankrupt you. Here's the honest beginner version.

How to read a single candle

The body is the range between open and close (green = close above open, red = below). The thin wicks show the high and low. Long wicks mean price was rejected there; big bodies mean strong conviction.

The few patterns worth knowing

  • Doji: open ≈ close (tiny body). Indecision — a possible pause or turn.
  • Hammer: small body, long lower wick. Buyers rejected lower prices — possible bottom.
  • Shooting star: small body, long upper wick. Sellers rejected higher prices — possible top.
  • Engulfing: a big candle that fully covers the previous one — a possible shift in control.

The honest truth about patterns

Patterns are probabilities, not predictions. The same hammer "works" and "fails" constantly. They only mean something with context (trend, support/resistance, volume) — and even then, your risk management matters far more than the pattern. A perfect setup with no stop still blows up.

Patterns don't lose you money — habits do

Most traders can spot a hammer. They still lose, because they oversize, skip stops and revenge-trade. The edge isn't the pattern; it's the discipline around it. ChintasMoney scores that discipline from your own trades. Start with risk management.
