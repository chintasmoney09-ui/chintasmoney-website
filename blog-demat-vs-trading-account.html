Demat account vs trading account: the difference

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

Two names that confuse every beginner. The difference is actually simple once you see what each one does.

Demat account = the locker

A demat (dematerialised) account holds your shares in electronic form. When you buy and take delivery, the shares sit here, like a digital locker for your holdings.

Trading account = the remote control

A trading account is what you use to place orders — to buy and sell on the exchange. It's the action layer that connects your bank, your demat and the market.

How they work together

You buy through the trading account → shares are delivered into your demat account → money comes from your bank account. For intraday and F&O, where you don't take delivery, the demat matters less day to day — but you still open both. Most brokers open them together in one process.

What a beginner needs

Both, plus a linked bank account and completed KYC — then you can trade. Choosing the right broker for these matters: see how to choose a broker.
