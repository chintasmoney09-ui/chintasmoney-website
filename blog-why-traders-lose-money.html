Why do most traders lose money in F&O?

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

If you've searched this, you already suspect the answer isn't "I need a better tip." SEBI's own study found that roughly 93% of individual F&O traders lost money over a recent three-year period, with average losses running into lakhs. That's not bad luck for a few people — it's a pattern. And a pattern has causes you can fix.

It's rarely the analysis. It's the behaviour.

Most losing traders can read a chart fine. What sinks them is what they do around the trade: entering without a stop, doubling down after a loss, booking winners too early and holding losers too long. The market doesn't take your money — your habits hand it over.

The 5 reasons traders actually lose

  1. No stop-loss. "I'll exit if it gets bad" is not a plan. One gap-down without a stop can erase a month of gains.
  2. Revenge trading. After a red trade, you fire back fast to "win it back" — and turn one loss into five. Here's how to stop it.
  3. Oversized positions. Risking 10–20% on one trade means three bad trades end you. The fix is the 1% risk rule.
  4. Cutting winners, holding losers. Fear books your +₹2,000 fast; hope lets your −₹6,000 run. The maths guarantees you lose.
  5. Overtrading. Boredom is not a setup. More trades = more fees + more mistakes, not more profit.

Why it feels invisible

Your broker app shows your P&L, never the reason. So you keep repeating the same mistake without a name for it. The traders who turn the corner are the ones who start measuring their discipline, not just their profit — because discipline is the one thing you fully control.

How to fix it (without a single tip)

Log every trade honestly — entry, stop, exit reason, and the emotion behind it. Within 20 trades your top two leaks become obvious, and fixing just those usually changes everything.
