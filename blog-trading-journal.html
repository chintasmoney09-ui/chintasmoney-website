How to keep a trading journal (and why it doubles your learning)

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

Every serious trader is told to "keep a journal," but almost nobody explains what to write or how to use it. A P&L screenshot isn't a journal — it tells you the score, not the reason. A real journal turns each trade into a lesson, so you stop repeating the mistakes that cost you.

What to log for every trade

  1. Symbol, date & direction — the basics.
  2. Entry, stop-loss & exit — and whether you actually set a stop.
  3. Setup — why you took it (breakout, reversal, news, or… boredom?).
  4. Exit reason — hit target, hit stop, booked early, held too long, revenge, FOMO.
  5. Emotion — calm, greedy, fearful, revengeful. This is the column that matters most, and the one everyone skips.

Why the emotion column changes everything

Numbers alone hide the truth. But when you tag emotions, patterns jump out: "Every big loss happened when I was on tilt after a red trade." That single insight is worth more than any indicator, because now you know the exact behaviour to fix.

How to review it (the part people skip)

Once a week, read your journal and ask three questions:

  • Which mistake shows up most often?
  • How much did it cost me this week?
  • What one rule would have prevented it?

Then make that one rule for next week. Improving one leak at a time compounds fast.
