How to set a stop-loss — and where to place it

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

A stop-loss is a pre-set exit that closes your trade when it moves against you by a defined amount. It's the single most important risk tool a trader has — and the one beginners most often skip or sabotage. Here's how to use it properly.

Why a stop-loss is non-negotiable

Without a stop, your "maximum loss" is your entire account. One gap-down, one fast move, one moment away from the screen, and a small loss becomes a catastrophic one. A stop converts an unknown, unlimited risk into a known, fixed one — which is the only way to size positions sanely.

Three common ways to place a stop

  1. Technical stop: just beyond a level the price "shouldn't" cross if you're right — below a support, above a resistance, beyond a swing high/low. Most logical, because it's tied to your trade idea.
  2. Percentage stop: a fixed % from entry (e.g. 1–2%). Simple, but ignores where the chart actually matters.
  3. Volatility stop: based on how much the instrument typically moves (e.g. using ATR), so you're not stopped out by normal noise.

Let the stop decide your size — not the other way around

Place the stop where your idea is invalidated first, then size the position so that distance equals your 1% risk. If the logical stop is far, trade fewer lots. Never tighten a stop just to buy more. The position size calculator links the two for you.

The one rule that matters most

Setting a stop is easy. Honouring it is hard. The classic account-killer is moving the stop wider "to give it room" as the trade goes against you — turning a planned ₹1,000 loss into ₹6,000. A stop you move is not a stop. Decide it before entry; let it do its job.
