Trading psychology: 6 biases that cost Indian traders money

Reading time: ~4 min · Educational — not investment advice

You can know every candlestick pattern and still lose — because trading is decided less by analysis and more by psychology. Your brain is wired for survival, not for markets, and those instincts backfire when real money is on the line. Here are the six biases that quietly drain accounts, and how to counter each.

1. Loss aversion

A loss hurts about twice as much as an equal gain feels good. So you book small winners fast (to feel safe) and let losers run (to avoid the pain of realising them). The result: small wins, big losses. Fix: set your target and stop before entering, and honour both.

2. Revenge (the tilt loop)

After a red trade, the urge to "win it back" fires instantly — bigger size, no plan. One loss becomes five. Fix: a hard rule — after a loss, step away for 10 minutes. More on beating revenge trading.

3. FOMO (fear of missing out)

A stock is flying, everyone's talking about it, so you jump in late — right as it reverses. Fix: if it's not your pre-defined setup, it's not your trade. Missing a move costs nothing; chasing one costs money.

4. Overconfidence after a win

Three green trades and you feel invincible, so you size up and get sloppy. Markets punish this fast. Fix: keep your risk per trade fixed regardless of your mood or streak.

5. Anchoring

You anchor to the price you bought at ("I'll exit when it comes back to my entry"), ignoring what the chart is actually saying now. Fix: decisions are based on your plan and stop, not your entry price.

6. Recency bias

A green week makes you brave; a red week makes you scared. Your risk should ignore your recent results. Fix: follow the same rules on day 1 and day 100.
