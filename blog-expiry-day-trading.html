Expiry day trading: what it is and why it's risky

Reading time: ~5 min · Educational — not investment advice

Expiry day is when option contracts end — and in India, with weekly index expiries, it comes around fast. It's also where beginners most often get wiped out. Here's why.

What happens on expiry

On the expiry date, options settle. Any time value left in the premium decays to near zero by the close — options finish worth only their intrinsic value (if any). Out-of-the-money options can go to zero.

Why it's so dangerous

  • Brutal theta decay: premiums bleed value fast through the day — buyers can be "right" on direction and still lose.
  • Violent, whippy moves: small index moves cause huge % swings in cheap options.
  • The lottery trap: cheap far-OTM options feel like a tempting bet — and usually expire worthless. It's gambling, not trading.

Why beginners get hurt

The low premiums make expiry feel "cheap," so people oversize and overtrade, chasing a jackpot. The emotion is intense and the decay is merciless — a perfect storm for broken discipline. See how to control emotions.

Respect the day, measure yourself

If you trade expiry, size tiny, set hard limits, and never chase. ChintasMoney flags oversized, overtrading-heavy sessions and scores your discipline — expiry day is exactly when you need that mirror most.
